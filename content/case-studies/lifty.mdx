## Problem

Workout apps either plan your session or watch you do it, rarely both. Without a coach, beginners don't know whether a squat is deep enough or their torso is leaning, and fixed weekly plans ignore how tired you are today.

Lifty is an iOS coach that plans the day's session **and** turns the front camera into a form coach that counts reps, scores each one and talks you through the set.

## My role

**Solo project** for the iOS Application Development course at Nanjing Xiaozhuang University (June 2026). I built the app end to end and **trained the squat-form model myself in PyTorch**, then converted it to Core ML.

## Architecture

Everything that touches the camera runs on the phone. Apple Vision finds the body joints. A rule engine reads joint angles to count reps, while a small sequence model classifies squat form from the last 60 frames.

## Technical highlights

- **Temporal model, not a single frame.** Form problems such as torso lean only show up over time, so the classifier reads a 60-frame sequence. Hip-centred normalisation makes it independent of where you stand.
- **Geometry rules for counting.** A squat rep counts when the knee angle reaches 110° or less and comes back to at least 160°, at most once every 0.5 s. Counting starts only after setup stays at 85% or higher for two seconds.
- **Explainable daily plan.** A rule-based engine scores candidate sessions by calorie fit, duration, intensity, novelty and equipment, applies a safety penalty, and explains its choice in plain language.
- **Camera work off the main thread.** Capture and analysis run on their own queues, and only published results reach SwiftUI.
- **Grounded coach chat.** Llama 3.3 70B on Groq receives a short summary of your profile and week, never your raw history.

## Results

- A working on-device form coach for squats, push-ups and planks, with spoken cues at four levels.
- **41 XCTest unit tests** covering the rep geometry, calibration, recommendation engine and chat context.
- A **320 KB** Core ML model that runs live on the phone.

## What I learned

Combining simple geometry rules with a small learned model worked better than either alone: the rules are reliable for counting, and the model catches subtler form faults. Next, I would train models for push-ups and planks the same way, and move the chat key behind a small backend proxy for a real release.
