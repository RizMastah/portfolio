## Problem

Indonesian fiscal and monetary policy is published in jargon and national totals. Households have no simple way to see what a fuel-price change, VAT increase or school-meal program means for **their own monthly budget**, or whether official numbers match what people see on the ground.

KawalBijak is a civic-tech platform that translates policy into **rupiah per month for your household profile**, then compares official figures with verified field reports from citizens.

## My role

I was **Backend Lead** in a team of three (team LaBuLaDa). I built:

- the **Formula Engine**, which computes each policy’s deterministic rupiah impact from a user’s profile
- the **Stats Engine**, which flags anomalous citizen reports with Z-score, IQR and geographic clustering
- the **API routes**, including the 8-step verification pipeline
- the **Groq integration** (Llama 3.3 70B), with guardrails and a public audit log

## Architecture

A Next.js 15 app on top of Supabase Postgres with Row Level Security on every table. Citizen reports pass through a fixed verification pipeline before they can influence any public number.

The database has **9 PostgreSQL tables**, each with Row Level Security, and two triggers. One creates a profile on sign-up. The other recalculates regional gap scores whenever a verification changes.

## Technical highlights

- **Deterministic numbers only.** Every figure shown to the public comes from a formula or a standard statistical method. The LLM is limited to four text tasks: summarizing policy, giving personal advice, checking whether a report’s text is plausible, and answering scoped Q&A.
- **Credibility-weighted gap score.** Official budget figures are compared with citizen reports, and each report is weighted by its reporter’s credibility.
- **Statistics before AI.** The more expensive LLM plausibility check runs only when the Z-score marks a report as unusual.
- **Auditable AI.** Every model call is logged with its prompt, parameters and output for public review.
- **Database-level consistency.** Gap scores are recalculated by a Postgres trigger, so the API can’t forget to update them.

## What I learned

Keeping the LLM away from the numbers made the system much easier to defend in front of judges: every rupiah value can be traced to a formula. Next, I would add a choropleth map per province and macro context (exchange rate, inflation) before opening a public API.
